About ReliefGrid

ReliefGrid helps you find shelter, food, healthcare, and other support services on Long Island. It is built to show whether a place has room — not just that it exists — once providers report their availability.

This is a prototype. Availability shown in ReliefGrid (open, beds, walk-ins, wait times) is simulated for demonstration. Always confirm directly with a provider before relying on it.

How AI is used

When you describe what you need, AI turns your words into a search (for example “shelter + food, needed now”). ReliefGrid then searches its own listings — the AI never invents services, addresses, hours or availability. You can see and change what it understood.

ReliefGrid does not collect or store any personal or identifying information about people seeking help, and does not store what you type. To interpret a request, the text is sent through ReliefGrid’s server to an AI provider — so please leave out names, phone numbers and other identifying details. Saved resources are kept only in your own browser, on your own device.

Data Transparency

Data & Methods

Community Need

A standardized composite of poverty rate, rent burden (paying ≥30% of income on rent), and renter share per census tract (2022 ACS 5-year estimates). Higher values indicate greater housing-instability risk.

Service Access

An Enhanced 2-Step Floating Catchment Area (E2SFCA) score: resources reachable within a 15-minute travel catchment, weighted by competing household demand, percentile-ranked 0–100.

Service Gap

Community Need minus Service Access (both standardized). Positive values mean need exceeds access. Statistically significant clusters are identified with Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA) at the 5% significance level — shown on the map as "Service Gap Clusters."

Resource network

182 shelters, food pantries, clinics, legal aid, and social-service locations across Nassau & Suffolk counties, compiled from county and nonprofit resource lists.

Availability (prototype)

Beds, meals, wait times, and open/closed status shown throughout ReliefGrid are simulated for a subset of resources, or entered through the demo Provider Updates page. See services/availability.js for the adapter architecture a real integration (provider APIs, HMIS, Bitfocus Clarity, 211, findhelp, Unite Us, government open data) would plug into.

AI assistance

AI is an interpretation layer, not a data source. The Resource Navigator converts a request into ReliefGrid’s existing categories and filters; results come from the listings above. Ask ReliefGrid can only read the precomputed tract, cluster and resource data through a fixed set of analysis tools; referenced areas are checked against that data and figures that can’t be matched are flagged. See docs/AI_ARCHITECTURE.md.

Limitations

  • Travel-time catchments are estimated, not live routing.
  • Availability is demo data unless a provider has updated it in this browser session.
  • Eligibility rules are not modeled per resource — always confirm with the provider.
  • McKinney-Vento school data (used in early research) reflects school-identified homelessness only; adults are undercounted.
  • Service Access is a single combined score per tract; it is not broken down by service category.
